ATLAS (BETA) · ABOUT

Explore research models in context.

Atlas is an interactive map for finding papers, comparing modeling ideas, and moving from a broad topic to the evidence inside a paper.

What Atlas is

Each point in the galaxy represents a research paper. Nearby points share a subject neighborhood and related modeling language. Search results and subject selections highlight parts of the same map rather than creating a different collection.

Each paper page combines bibliographic information with a structured reading note. Depending on the available evidence, a note may describe the research question, model setup, assumptions, components, notation, formulations, and source passages.

How to use Atlas

  1. Browse the galaxy. Choose a subject to highlight its neighborhood. Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, then select a paper point to open it.
  2. Search or compare. Search for a modeling idea, title, author, or DOI. Open Panels when you want a list that can be filtered and compared.
  3. Inspect the paper. Read the structured note and its source references, then use the DOI or Google Scholar link to check the publisher’s version.

About the Beta version

Atlas is currently in Beta, which means it is a testing version. The interface, classifications, search ranking, extracted text, and structured notes are still being checked and improved.

Atlas is a research aid, not a substitute for reading the paper. A paper may be placed imperfectly, and a note may omit context or preserve an extraction error. Verify definitions, equations, assumptions, proofs, and results against the original publication before relying on them.

The collection is selective rather than exhaustive. The articles remain subject to their publishers’ and authors’ copyright and licensing terms.
